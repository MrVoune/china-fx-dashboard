What the latest data say

    How to read this page

    • Two different conventions for the exchange rate. USD/CNY is quoted as yuan per 1 US dollar: a rising line means the RMB is weakening. BIS effective exchange rate indices (REER, NEER) are the opposite: a rising line means the RMB is strengthening.
    • Latest observation vs refresh time. Each chart shows the date of its newest data point; the page header shows when the job last ran successfully. Sources publish at different lags, so a successful refresh may add no new points.
    • Uncovered interest parity (UIP) check. Implied change = (1 + yCN) / (1 + yUS) − 1 using 10-year yields; realised change = USD/CNY twelve months later vs now. A gap between the two is the "carry" that UIP says should not persist.
    • Real exchange rate. BIS REER deflates trade-weighted nominal rates with relative consumer prices, so it moves with both the nominal rate and the China–partner inflation gap. Productivity growth differentials matter for the long-run real rate (Balassa–Samuelson).
    • NBS PMI and GDP are read from the National Bureau of Statistics English release pages (its data portal blocks automated access); history starts where those pages are available.
